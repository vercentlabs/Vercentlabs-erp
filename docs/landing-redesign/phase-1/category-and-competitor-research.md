## Pattern matrix

| Pattern | Strong examples | Why it works | Risk | Vercentlabs adaptation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Product-led hero with real UI, not illustration | Linear, Stripe, Ramp | Buyers trust what they can see the system actually do; abstract hero art reads as "nothing to show" | Screenshots age, need real maintenance; can look cluttered if not curated | Adopt directly — Direction A's annotated-screenshot hero is this pattern, disciplined for ERP's data density (see [[creative-direction]]) |
| Mega-menu organized by buyer mental model, not internal product structure | Salesforce, ServiceNow, HubSpot | Reduces cognitive load; buyer finds themselves in the menu instead of hunting for a feature name | Can balloon into an overwhelming wall of links (Salesforce/SAP are frequently criticized for this) | Group by buyer-facing domain (Revenue/Operations/Finance/People/Delivery) per [[information-architecture]], capped at ~5 groups, not 12 flat module links |
| Industry/solution pages as the primary conversion surface, not just modules | NetSuite, Acumatica, Epicor | ERP buyers self-identify by industry before they think in module names | Thin industry pages that just relabel the same content hurt more than help | Only 3 industry pages, each genuinely different in module emphasis and pain framing — see [[icp-and-buyer-map]]; reject Epicor/Acumatica's pattern of a dozen+ thin industry pages |
| Interactive/self-serve product tour before a sales call | HubSpot, Stripe, Rippling | Lets a self-directed buyer build conviction without a call, shortens the funnel for high-intent visitors | Expensive to build well; a bad interactive demo is worse than a good screenshot | Planned as `/product-tour`, but scoped in Phase 1 IA only as a URL + purpose, not built — real interactivity is a later-phase build once real screenshots/recordings exist |
| Minimal, confident navigation with almost no visual noise | Linear | Signals product maturity and design discipline; very fast, very legible | Can read as "too simple for enterprise" to a risk-averse ERP buying committee if taken too far, and is explicitly named in the brief as something not to copy wholesale (Linear's black aesthetic specifically) | Borrow the *discipline*, not the dark aesthetic — Direction A keeps a light, structured surface instead |
| Heavy use of customer logos and named case studies above the fold | Salesforce, HubSpot, Rippling, ServiceNow | Fast trust transfer for a buyer who doesn't yet know the company | Requires real customers; fabricating this is explicitly prohibited by the Evidence and Honesty Rules | **Not used** until real customer evidence exists — substitute real product screenshots and a transparent "what's built" module-by-module page (see [[positioning-and-messaging]] objection responses) |
| Named implementation methodology as a first-class page | Acumatica, NetSuite (partner-delivery model), SAP | Directly answers the #1 ERP objection ("implementations fail") | Can read as generic ("Discover, Design, Deploy...") if not specific | `/implementation` is P0 in the IA specifically to counter this objection with an honest, specific description, not a templated 3-step graphic |
| Security/trust page as a dedicated, deep page (not a footer link) | Stripe, Rippling, ServiceNow | Buying-committee members (IT, legal) search for this specifically late in the funnel — a shallow page loses a real, high-intent visitor | Easy to pad with generic "bank-level security" language that reads as filler | `/security` is P0; content must cite real implemented controls (roles/permissions, audit trail, data isolation — see [[product-intelligence]]) not generic security marketing copy |
| Transparent, self-serve pricing or clear "how pricing works" page even without exact numbers | Stripe, Ramp, HubSpot | Reduces the "contact us to find out" friction that frustrates evaluators early in the funnel | ERP pricing is genuinely often variable (seats, modules, implementation) — a naive fixed-price table would misrepresent that | `/pricing` (P0) explains the *shape* of pricing (what drives cost) honestly rather than either hiding behind "Contact Us" or inventing a fake price table |
| Dense mega-menu feature walls exposing every SKU/module/sub-feature | SAP, older Oracle properties | Reflects true product breadth but overwhelms and reads as enterprise-bureaucratic | Directly named in the brief as a pattern to avoid ("exposing all 1,039 features in the main menu") | Rejected outright — nav stays at the module/group level per [[information-architecture]] |
| Workflow-as-narrative content (a named end-to-end process explained step by step) | Less common as a dedicated page type among the named competitors (most bury this inside feature pages) | This is a genuine white-space opportunity — most ERP marketing sites describe modules, few describe the actual cross-module sequence a buyer's business runs | Requires real cross-module evidence to be credible, not just a diagram | Adopted as its own IA tier (`/workflows/*`) specifically because it's underused by the category and matches this product's real, evidenced cross-module data flow (see [[product-intelligence]]) |

## Category conventions to follow

- Product-led hero with real UI (Linear/Stripe/Ramp pattern).
- Industry pages as a primary, not secondary, conversion surface (NetSuite/Acumatica pattern) — kept intentionally small (3 pages) rather than the sprawling version some competitors run.
- A dedicated, substantive security page (Stripe/Rippling/ServiceNow pattern).
- A named, specific implementation-methodology page (Acumatica/NetSuite pattern), addressing the category's #1 objection directly.
- Self-serve product understanding before a sales call (HubSpot/Stripe pattern), scoped realistically to what Phase 1-5 can actually build.

## Category conventions to reject

- Dense, feature-exposing mega-menus (SAP/older-Oracle pattern) — explicitly prohibited by the brief and rejected here.
- Logo-wall/testimonial-heavy trust sections — not available honestly yet; substituted with product evidence per the Evidence and Honesty Rules.
- Thin, templated industry pages that just swap a noun (common among mid-market ERP vendors chasing SEO volume) — the IA caps industry pages at 3 genuinely differentiated ones.
- Copying any single competitor's visual identity wholesale — explicitly prohibited by the brief (no SAP/Salesforce navigation, no Linear black aesthetic, no Stripe gradients) and addressed structurally in [[creative-direction]].

## White-space opportunities

1. **Workflow-as-narrative pages** are genuinely underused by the named category incumbents — most competitors describe modules in isolation and leave the buyer to infer how they connect. Vercentlabs' real, evidenced cross-module data flow (see [[product-intelligence]]) makes this a defensible, differentiated content type rather than a marketing conceit.
2. **Honest scope transparency** ("here's exactly what's built in this module today") is essentially unused across the category — everyone's module pages read as a complete, idealized feature list. A module page that's plainly organized by real capability groups, without inflating scope, is a credibility differentiator for a company at this evidence stage (no fabricated logos yet).
3. **Making the real product accent-color system visible** (per module) as a legitimate design device (a Direction A/C hybrid idea, carried into A per [[creative-direction]]) is not a pattern any named competitor uses in quite this way — most competitors use one brand color throughout and differentiate modules only by icon or copy.

## Credibility-without-imitation strategy

Given zero real customer evidence exists yet (per the Evidence and Honesty Rules), credibility is built through: (1) real, annotated product UI wherever a claim is made, (2) transparent, specific implementation-methodology and security pages instead of vague trust badges, (3) workflow pages that prove cross-module integration with real evidence rather than asserting "connected platform" as a slogan, and (4) restraint — a smaller, denser, more evidenced site (per the IA's page caps) reads as more credible than a sprawling site trying to look bigger than it is.
